# Abstract

Face anti-spoofing (FAS) literature is dominated by per-frame classification, yet the contexts driving face-liveness demand — proctoring, hosted KYC, identity verification — are intrinsically *session-level*: an attacker can hold a screen-replay or printed photograph for minutes, briefly reveal a real face for one frame, and continue. We present **`spoof-detector` v0.2.1**, a session-based hybrid PAD engine combining a strong per-frame discriminator (MiniFASNet ONNX) with twelve auxiliary analyzers — image-level (device-boundary, AR-filter, texture, Gabor-moire) and temporal (blink/EAR, rPPG, screen-replay, micro-tremor, landmark-variance, temporal-motion, background-grid, screen-flicker) — under a calibrated multi-class fuser and a **peak-sensitive session verdict** (50/50 blend of mean and worst-decile liveness) that resists spoof-burst dilution. We evaluate zero-shot on two public benchmarks via HuggingFace mirrors — CASIA-FASD (N=2,408) and CelebA-Spoof (N=2,611) — plus an in-house replay sub-protocol (N=100), and report ISO/IEC 30107-3 APCER / BPCER / ACER and AUC with stratified bootstrap 95% confidence intervals. Headline: AUC 0.945 [0.937, 0.956] on CASIA-FASD and 0.782 [0.766, 0.799] on CelebA-Spoof; the two CIs are strictly separated by more than 4× their width, empirically nailing the cross-dataset taxonomy effect. Two structural findings: (i) Laplacian-texture and Gabor-moire are *anti-correlated* on modern high-resolution capture, and re-weighting them from 1.0 to 0.1 alone recovers 0.017 of the 0.019 AUC gap between uniform and calibrated fusers; (ii) three auxiliary analyzers calibrated on in-house data *actively harm* zero-shot performance on CASIA-FASD, motivating per-operator recalibration. An iBeta PAD-Level-1 submission package and MIT-licensed benchmark harness accompany the release.
