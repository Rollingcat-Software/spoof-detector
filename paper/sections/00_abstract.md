# Abstract

Face anti-spoofing (FAS) literature dominated by per-frame classification ignores the temporal dimension intrinsic to real-world attacks. We present **a session-based hybrid image-and-video presentation-attack detection (PAD) engine** that combines a strong per-frame discriminator (MiniFASNet ONNX) with seven temporal analyzers (blink, remote-photoplethysmography, screen flicker, micro-tremor, landmark variance, screen-replay, temporal consistency) under a calibrated multi-class fuser, and accumulates evidence into a peak-sensitive session verdict. We map per-analyzer performance to a 7-category spoof taxonomy (real, print, replay, 3-D mask, heavy makeup, AR filter, deepfake injection) and report ISO/IEC 30107-3 metrics on four established benchmarks (OULU-NPU, SiW, CASIA-SURF, CelebA-Spoof) plus an in-house 43-sample calibration set. Two findings stand out. First, two canonical anti-spoof signals (Gabor moire and Laplacian texture) are *anti-correlated* on real-world data — their suppression alone produced the largest single-step ACER improvement in our weight calibration. Second, the peak-sensitive session verdict (a 50/50 blend of mean and worst-window live-ness) prevents spoof-burst dilution in mixed sessions, a property critical for proctoring use cases where any sustained spoof must be flagged. We additionally release the consolidated research repository (`spoof-detector` v0.2.1) — the canonical home for every algorithm in the FIVUCSAS programme — and a reproducible benchmark harness implementing all four standard FAS protocols. Code, weights, and (consented) in-house data are open-sourced under MIT.
