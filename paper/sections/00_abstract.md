# Abstract

Face anti-spoofing (FAS) literature is dominated by per-frame classification, yet the contexts driving face-liveness demand — proctoring, hosted KYC, identity verification — are intrinsically *session-level*: an attacker can hold a screen-replay or printed photograph for minutes, briefly reveal a real face for one frame, and continue. We present **`spoof-detector`**, an open-source session-based hybrid PAD engine whose **production deliverable is a client-side browser bundle** running entirely on WebAssembly via `onnxruntime-web` (with optional WebGPU execution provider) and MediaPipe Tasks — no GPU server, no frame uploads, no native runtime. The browser engine combines a per-frame discriminator (MiniFASNet ONNX) with **nineteen auxiliary analyzers** — image-level (device-boundary, texture, Gabor-moire, screen-replay), temporal (blink/EAR, rPPG, screen-flicker, micro-tremor, landmark-variance, temporal-motion, background-grid), blendshape-derived (eyebrow motion, per-eye blink symmetry, gaze, expression dynamics), 3D-pose, behavioural-pattern, and optionally hand-presence and audio-mouth-sync — under a calibrated multi-class fuser and a **peak-sensitive session verdict** (50/50 blend of mean and worst-decile liveness, Fig. 1) that resists spoof-burst dilution. A **fifteen-axis passive liveness-proof scorer** with a 185-point ceiling tracks "is this user still live?" continuously through the session without active challenges, decaying naturally on stale signal so a live-then-static attack is caught even mid-session. A Python reference implementation — used for the formal evaluation reported here because calibration sweeps run faster outside the browser — is calibration-equivalent to the browser bundle for the analyzers present in both. We evaluate zero-shot on two public benchmarks via HuggingFace mirrors — CASIA-FASD (N=2,408) and CelebA-Spoof (N=2,611) — plus an in-house replay sub-protocol (N=100), and report ISO/IEC 30107-3 APCER / BPCER / ACER and AUC with stratified bootstrap 95% confidence intervals. Headline: AUC 0.945 [0.937, 0.956] on CASIA-FASD and 0.782 [0.766, 0.799] on CelebA-Spoof; the two CIs are strictly separated by more than 4× their width, empirically nailing the cross-dataset taxonomy effect. Two structural findings: (i) Laplacian-texture and Gabor-moire are *anti-correlated* on modern high-resolution capture, and re-weighting them from 1.0 to 0.1 alone recovers 0.017 of the 0.019 AUC gap between uniform and calibrated fusers; (ii) two auxiliary analyzers (device-boundary, micro-tremor) calibrated on in-house data *harm zero-shot AUC* on CASIA-FASD — leave-one-out AUC improvements when removed of 0.027 and 0.021 respectively — with metric-dependent ACER effects, motivating per-operator recalibration; a third in-house-calibrated analyzer (background-grid) is the lone marginal positive contributor (AUC drops 0.014 when removed). The live tester at `https://amispoof.fivucsas.com/` runs the full bundle in any modern browser at 6.7–9.5 fps on Pixel-class mobile hardware and 25–30 fps on desktop Chrome with WebGPU. An iBeta PAD-Level-1 submission package and MIT-licensed benchmark harness accompany the release.
