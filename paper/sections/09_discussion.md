# 9. Discussion

§9.1 maps the §7 and §8 results back onto the three structural gaps from §1. §9.2 reads the headline cross-dataset AUC separation through the §3 taxonomy. §9.3 discusses the calibration-transfer caveat from §8.2 and the tooling the harness provides operators to recalibrate locally. §9.4 catalogues limitations; §9.5 lists open problems.

## 9.1 Per-gap mapping

The four contributions from §1 each address one of the three motivating gaps. The §7 and §8 evidence supports them with measurably different strengths.

**Gap 1 (per-frame bias) is addressed by §4.4's peak-sensitive session verdict.** The published aggregator `P_session(REAL) = 0.5·mean + 0.5·worst-decile` (§4.4) is designed so that briefly revealing a real face inside an otherwise-spoof session cannot dilute the session live-ness above the spoof minutes. The architectural property is empirically supported on still-image protocols where the aggregator reduces to the per-frame fuser without regression: `hybrid` and `image_only` are statistically indistinguishable on the in-house replay sub-protocol (§7.2, both AUC 0.9264) and on both public datasets (§7.1, CASIA-FASD `image_only` 0.9140 vs `hybrid` 0.9139; CelebA-Spoof `image_only` 0.7262 vs `hybrid` 0.7245). The §4.3 "fuser does not regress beneath either input track" theorem is therefore empirically nailed across three independent protocols. The session-mixing component — that a 50-real / 10-spoof / 60-real session is flagged spoof — remains under-evaluated because the public-dataset HuggingFace mirrors provide still-image samples rather than multi-segment session videos; §8.4's mixed-session column is [TBD] pending the OULU-NPU / SiW EULAs. Evidence strength: **architectural property nailed, session-mixing property pending video data**.

**Gap 2 (single-modality models) is addressed by the §4 hybrid architecture's twelve auxiliary analyzers around the MiniFASNet per-frame discriminator.** The §8.2 leave-one-out measures discriminator dominance: removing MiniFASNet costs Δ-ACER +9.17 pp and Δ-AUC −0.1462 on the in-house set, and on CASIA-FASD costs Δ-ACER +16.37 pp and Δ-AUC −0.3079. The strong per-frame discriminator does most of the work; the auxiliary analyzers add second-order discrimination — `device_boundary` at +1.72 pp ACER and `background_grid` at +0.86 pp ACER on the in-house set (§8.2) — and the auxiliary bank does not regress the fused verdict beneath the MiniFASNet baseline on any in-distribution protocol. The "image + temporal + fused" thesis is corroborated in the controlled-distribution case but inverted out-of-distribution (§9.3). Evidence strength: **structurally validated; per-modality contribution bounded by the leave-one-out table**.

**Gap 3 (anti-correlated signals) is addressed by §5's calibration finding and §8.3's ablation.** §5.3's measurement — `texture` discrimination gap −6.3 and `moire` −5.0, inverted from the textbook positive coefficient — is the empirical anti-correlation. §8.3 closes the loop quantitatively: re-weighting `texture` and `moire` from 1.0 to 0.1 alone, all other weights held at uniform 1.0, recovers AUC 0.9472 versus the calibrated default's 0.9497 and the uniform-weights baseline's 0.9303 — recovering 0.017 of the 0.019 AUC gap between uniform and calibrated fusers, i.e. 89% of the calibration benefit captured by re-weighting two analyzers. The mechanism (modern OLED capture produces uniform sub-pixel texture and absent moire on rephotograph) is described in §5.3 and is independent of our specific implementation. Evidence strength: **finding empirically dominant — the single highest-leverage calibration decision**.

## 9.2 Cross-dataset taxonomy effect

The §7.1 headline is the strict separation of two AUC confidence intervals on the same `minifasnet_only` pipeline: AUC 0.9452 [0.9366, 0.9560] on CASIA-FASD (N=2,408) and AUC 0.7818 [0.7663, 0.7993] on CelebA-Spoof (N=2,611). The CIs are non-overlapping by 0.14 AUC points — more than 4× the width of either — so the cross-dataset gap is established at 95% confidence. What the §3 taxonomy tells us about why.

CASIA-FASD's attack taxonomy ([Zhang 2012], §2.3) is `warped photo`, `cut photo`, and `video replay` against `real`, collapsing cleanly onto two of our seven attack categories (PRINT and REPLAY, §3.2 and §3.4). MiniFASNet's UniFace training corpus ([Liu 2020b], §2.2) was assembled from public-PAD-corpus mixtures dominated by exactly these two attack species, so the zero-shot evaluation is effectively *within* the categories the per-frame discriminator has seen at training time. The 87% per-sample classification rate (computed from the 12.67% ACER, §7.1) is the result.

CelebA-Spoof's ten-class structure ([Zhang 2020], §2.3) spans `photo`, `poster`, `a4_paper`, `face_mask`, `upper_body_mask`, `region_mask`, `pc_screen`, `pad_screen`, `phone_screen`, and `3d_mask` (§3.4). Mapping onto our seven-class taxonomy: codes 1–3 → PRINT, codes 7–9 → REPLAY, codes 4–6 and 10 → MASK_3D. The MASK_3D category is the structural addition relative to CASIA-FASD, and our pipeline's coverage of MASK_3D is explicitly the weakest of the seven (§9.3 below) because the rPPG analyzer that would corroborate "no skin pulse → mask" is calibrated at weight 0.0 in the headline pipeline. The 0.16-AUC gap between the two datasets is therefore not principally a property of the *data* — it is a property of the *intersection between the data's spoof distribution and the analyzer bank's calibrated coverage*. CASIA-FASD's attack space is inside the analyzer bank's competence; CelebA-Spoof's attack space includes four mask sub-codes the bank does not cover at zero-shot calibration. The §3.4 mapping table makes this explicit and is the lens through which we recommend any future cross-dataset FAS evaluation be read.

## 9.3 Calibration-transfer caveat

The §8.2 cross-dataset leave-one-out on CASIA-FASD is, in our view, the most operationally important finding in the paper. Two auxiliary analyzers (`device_boundary`, `micro_tremor`) calibrated on the in-house Marmara set actively *harm* zero-shot AUC on CASIA-FASD. Removing `device_boundary` improves ACER by 2.69 pp and AUC by 0.027; removing `micro_tremor` improves AUC by 0.021 (it raises ACER by 3.60 pp when removed, so the effect is metric-dependent). A third in-house-calibrated analyzer, `background_grid`, is a marginal *positive* contributor on this dataset — removing it reduces AUC by 0.014 with no ACER change — and is the lone in-house-calibrated auxiliary that retains its discriminative value out-of-distribution. The mechanism behind the two harmful analyzers is mundane and shared: each one's calibrated decision threshold was learned against 2026-era phone-bezel patterns (`device_boundary`) or 8–12 Hz tripod-mounted-laptop micro-tremor signatures (`micro_tremor`); the 2012-era CASIA-FASD captures exhibit none of those patterns, so the analyzers' "spoof" evidence fires on bona-fide captures and contaminates the fused verdict.

Two consequences for operators.

First, **deployment without per-operator recalibration is structurally unsafe.** The §5.5 recommendation — re-run the calibration sweep on a new operator's labelled data, with ≥30 bona-fide and ≥15 attacks per class — is not advisory; the §8.2 numbers establish that calibration coefficients carry hard distribution-specific assumptions that silently invert under domain shift. The pipeline ships with calibrated defaults *because* the in-house set's distribution is internally consistent; those defaults are explicitly not portable to operator-side capture domains.

Second, **the harness provides the tooling for operators to recalibrate locally.** `tests/benchmark/calibration_sweep.py` (§5.4) performs the 1-D weight sweep over each analyzer's coefficient on a labelled operator set and emits the per-coefficient ACER plateau; `tests/benchmark/ablation_leave_one_out.py` (§8.2) computes per-analyzer Δ-ACER and Δ-AUC tables on the operator's evaluation split, surfacing exactly the harm-on-transfer pattern §8.2 reports for CASIA-FASD; `src/infrastructure/fusion/multi_class_fuser.py:26-40` is the single file edited once the sweep selects new coefficients — no model retraining, no analyzer code change, no architectural change. §5.6's claim that "the only learned numbers are 13 floats in a Python dict" is exactly what makes per-operator recalibration tractable.

## 9.4 Limitations

**Sample size relative to industry-scale deployments.** The headline results use N=2,408 (CASIA-FASD test split) and N=2,611 (CelebA-Spoof eval shard 0); the corresponding AUC CI widths from §7.1 are 0.0194 and 0.0330. These widths are narrow enough to establish the §9.2 4× cross-dataset separation at 95% confidence but remain an order of magnitude wider than what an industry-scale evaluation against millions of authentication attempts would produce. §8.7's N-effect table illustrates the CI-tightening rate; a 10× larger evaluation would tighten widths by roughly √10 ≈ 3.2× and catch second-order analyzer interactions invisible here.

**No OULU-NPU or SiW results.** Both benchmarks are operator-blocked on dataset EULAs (CMVS Oulu and MSU respectively, §6.1) we have not yet acquired. OULU-NPU's four protocols are the de-facto FAS robustness standard ([Boulkenafet 2017], §2.3); SiW's 165-subject pose-and-lighting variation is the canonical session-length test bed ([Liu 2018], §2.3). The session-length curve §8.6 and the peak-sensitive vs mean ablation §8.4 are both [TBD] pending these EULAs.

**AR-filter classifier is operator-data-collection-blocked.** The MobileNetV3-Small classifier flagged in §3.2's `AR_FILTER` category requires 500+ samples per filter type across at least five families (Snapchat, Instagram, TikTok, FaceApp, OBS Virtual Camera). The published pipeline uses the heuristic `ar_filter_analyzer` at weight 0.3 (§3.3, Table 1); §7.3's 56.00% APCER on the `ar_filter` sub-protocol is dominated by synthesiser limitations (§6.4) rather than classifier limitations, but the upgrade path remains data-blocked.

**α = 0.5 in the peak-sensitive verdict is a heuristic.** The 50/50 mean / worst-decile blend in §4.4 was selected for proctoring deployments where spoof-burst dilution is the dominant failure mode. Per-operator session-length distributions, attack-burst statistics, and BPCER tolerances should drive an automatically-tuned α; we have not yet built that tuner. A first-pass version would minimise mixed-session ACER on a held-out operator labelled set.

**iBeta PAD-Level-2 is not certified in this paper.** The §1 contribution claim is the open-source release plus an iBeta PAD-Level-1 *submission package* (`iBeta_PAD_Level1_Submission_Package.md`). Level 2 — which our pipeline targets architecturally via the active-challenge layer (§4.5) and the rPPG / micro-tremor temporal channels — requires the operator to physically attend an iBeta test facility and pass under iBeta's confidential protocol. We report it as a forward-looking commitment rather than a measured result.

## 9.5 Open problems and future work

Five open problems, ordered by tractability.

1. **Multi-frame analyzer benchmarks on video data.** §8.2's leave-one-out shows nine of the twelve auxiliary analyzers contributing 0% Δ-ACER on still-image protocols because they require ≥30-frame buffers. The peak-sensitive aggregator (§8.4) cannot be measured against spoof-burst dilution without multi-segment video sessions. Both gaps close once OULU-NPU and SiW are accessible.

2. **Active-challenge integration.** The active layer in §4.5 (colour-flash, head-rotation, blink-on-command) reportedly adds 30–50 percentage-point swings on the hardest replay attacks (§8.5), but the FlashSpoofAnalyzer synthesis smoke test yields only AUC 0.5685 (§8.5) because every sample is rendered with the same diffuse-flash response. Real pre/flash pairs from consented users + replay attacks would convert §8.5's TBD rows into paper-grade numbers.

3. **AR-filter classifier data collection.** §9.4 above. The 500+ × 5-filter-type capture campaign is the gating step; classifier architecture and integration path through `src/infrastructure/analyzers/ar_filter_analyzer.py` are in place.

4. **Cross-dataset α tuning.** An auto-tuner that minimises mixed-session ACER on a held-out operator set, parameterised over α and the per-analyzer weights jointly. The §5.4 sweep harness is the launching point.

5. **iBeta PAD-Level-2 in-person certification.** Once the Level-1 submission package is accepted under the protocol pinned in the v0.2.1 release commit, scheduling Level-2 attendance certifies the architectural claims this paper makes.
