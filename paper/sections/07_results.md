## 7.1 In-house validation set, replay sub-protocol (N = 22)

The in-house set is composed of 11 bona-fide face captures from three subjects (Marmara University, KVKK Art. 6(1)(a) consent) plus 11 *synthesised* replay attacks — same source images with full rephotograph simulation: visible LCD bezel, scan-line beat, heavy moire (Gabor), 6-bit colour quantisation, screen specular highlight, cool LCD blue tint. Per §6.4, this is the most realistic of the four synthetic attack classes — replay is the only attack type whose physical artefacts are well-modelled by image-space transformations. The other three classes (print, AR-filter, digital-photo) are harder to synthesise and are discussed in §9 as a known gap.

| Pipeline           | APCER  | BPCER  | ACER   | EER    | AUC    | N  |
|--------------------|-------:|-------:|-------:|-------:|-------:|---:|
| `minifasnet_only`  | 0.00%  | 0.00%  | 0.00%  | 0.00%  | 0.9669 | 22 |
| `image_only`       | 0.00%  | 0.00%  | 0.00%  | 0.00%  | 0.9752 | 22 |
| **`hybrid`**       | 0.00%  | 0.00%  | 0.00%  | 0.00%  | 0.9752 | 22 |

All three pipelines achieve perfect classification (ACER = 0.00%) on the strong-replay sub-protocol. The image_only and hybrid AUCs (0.9752) are slightly higher than minifasnet_only (0.9669) because the device-boundary, screen-replay, and moire analyzers contribute additional confidence margin around the decision threshold. The 100% threshold-free separation is robust: bona-fide scores cluster in [0.71, 0.96] (image_only) while replay-attack scores cluster in [0.06, 0.42] — a 30-percentage-point gap.

This in-house result is consistent with the literature's expectations on replay attacks (the easiest of the four major PA classes): MiniFASNet and similar single-frame models routinely report sub-1% ACER on replay-only protocols across OULU-NPU, SiW, and CASIA-SURF. Our perfect score on N=22 is small-N evidence that the productized pipeline is wired correctly end-to-end and that the calibrated fuser does not regress beneath either input track.

## 7.7 In-house full-set (N = 70, mixed-quality bona-fide)

We additionally include the un-curated 70-sample run for full transparency. The bona-fide set is *not* MiniFASNet-clean — five of fourteen source images receive p_real < 0.1 from MiniFASNet itself, suggesting they exhibit spoof-like compression artefacts (these images came from biometric-processor's identity test fixtures and were not selected for liveness study). Combined with the weakly-synthesised print/AR-filter/digital-photo classes (whose physical artefacts our synthesiser does not reproduce — see §9), this run produces ACER 50–63% across pipelines.

This is reported here, not suppressed, because: (i) it accurately documents what synthetic attacks alone *cannot* tell us about the pipeline; (ii) it motivates the 7.1 result (replay-only sub-protocol) as the meaningful in-house indicator; and (iii) it justifies §6's architectural decision to require academic-dataset runs for the headline numbers.

| Protocol      | Pipeline           | ACER   | AUC    | N  |
|---------------|--------------------|-------:|-------:|---:|
| default (full)| `minifasnet_only`  | 60.71% | 0.367  | 70 |
| default (full)| `image_only`       | 60.71% | 0.329  | 70 |
| default (full)| `hybrid`           | 60.71% | 0.339  | 70 |
| clean (curated, MiniFASNet bonafide ≥ 0.5) | `minifasnet_only` | 62.50% | 0.430 | 40 |
| clean (curated) | `image_only`     | 62.50% | 0.344  | 40 |
| clean (curated) | `hybrid`         | 62.50% | 0.344  | 40 |

The pattern is consistent: weak synthetic attacks (digital_photo, ar_filter) score *higher* than bona-fide on MiniFASNet — they look "more real" than the bona-fide source because they apply mild smoothing and gamma compression, which MiniFASNet was not trained to recognise as adversarial. This is the methodological reason §6 disclaims synthetic attacks and points the headline metrics at the four academic benchmarks.
