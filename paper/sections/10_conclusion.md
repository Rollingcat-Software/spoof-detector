# 10. Conclusion

We have presented `spoof-detector` v0.2.1, a session-based hybrid presentation-attack detection engine for the deployment contexts — proctoring, hosted KYC, regulated remote-onboarding — that the per-frame FAS literature does not directly serve. We have shown that the four contributions from §1 hold under controlled evaluation. We have presented a hybrid architecture (§4) that combines one strong per-frame discriminator (MiniFASNet ONNX, fuser weight 5.0) with twelve auxiliary analyzers across image and temporal modalities, fused under a calibrated multi-class voter and aggregated by a peak-sensitive 50/50 mean-plus-worst-decile session verdict (§4.4) designed to resist spoof-burst dilution. We have measured the principal empirical finding — that Laplacian-texture and Gabor-moire are anti-correlated on modern high-resolution capture (§5.3) — and shown in a measured weight-configuration study (§8.3, N=83) that the calibrated fuser improves AUC by +0.0234 over uniform weighting (0.9190 → 0.9424), with re-weighting those two analyzers from 1.0 to 0.1 alone recovering ≈65 % of that gain (partial config 0.9341): a single two-coefficient adjustment captures the bulk of the calibration benefit.

We have presented bootstrap-CI ISO/IEC 30107-3 evaluation on two zero-shot public benchmarks: AUC 0.9452 [0.9366, 0.9560] on CASIA-FASD (N=2,408) and AUC 0.7818 [0.7663, 0.7993] on CelebA-Spoof (N=2,611). The two confidence intervals are strictly separated by more than 4× their width, establishing the cross-dataset taxonomy effect at 95% confidence. We have additionally measured (§8.2) that two auxiliary analyzers calibrated on the in-house Marmara set actively harm zero-shot AUC on CASIA-FASD — `device_boundary` by 0.027 and `micro_tremor` by 0.021 — while `background_grid` is the lone in-house-calibrated auxiliary that transfers as a marginal positive contributor (AUC drops 0.014 when removed); together these motivate per-operator recalibration as a structural requirement of any cross-domain deployment.

We have released the engine, the calibration sweep harness, the leave-one-out ablation harness, the four-dataset adapters, and the ISO/IEC 30107-3 metrics implementation under MIT, alongside an iBeta PAD-Level-1 submission package pinned to the v0.2.1 release commit. The actionable artefact for the FAS community is the open benchmark harness itself: per-operator recalibration, leave-one-out diagnosis of harm-on-transfer, and bootstrap-CI ISO-compliant reporting are now one-command operations on any labelled FAS corpus. We expect the next round of public FAS benchmarks — and the next generation of cross-dataset robustness studies — to take advantage of that infrastructure.

## 10.1 The browser engine as primary deliverable

The session-PAD engine shipped here is, first and concretely, a **browser bundle** running entirely in the user's tab: `@rollingcat/spoof-detector` on npm, live at `https://amispoof.fivucsas.com/`, ~173 kB ESM / 54 kB gzip, no GPU server, no frames uploaded, no native runtime. We chose this deployment topology in response to Gap 4 (§1): the dominant face-PAD topology in the literature assumes a server-side GPU service, and that assumption carries hard structural costs (GDPR/KVKK consent on each uploaded frame, per-session GPU rent, service-availability dependence). The browser engine inverts the topology so the operator pays nothing per session for inference and the user's biometric never leaves the device.

As of the 2026-05-17 build the browser bundle runs **nineteen analyzers**: the original ten from §4.1 (MiniFASNet, screen-flicker, micro-tremor, blink/EAR, rPPG, device-boundary, landmark-variance, moire, texture, screen-replay) plus seven blendshape- and 3D-pose-derived analyzers unlocked when MediaPipe FaceLandmarker is run with `outputFaceBlendshapes` and `outputFacialTransformationMatrixes` enabled (`EyebrowAnalyzer`, `BlinkSymmetryAnalyzer`, `GazeAnalyzer`, `ExpressionDynamicsAnalyzer`, `Pose3DConsistencyAnalyzer`, `BehavioralPatternAnalyzer`, plus `TemporalAnalyzer` and `BackgroundGridAnalyzer` brought over from the Python reference), and two opt-in heavy analyzers (`BackgroundMotionAnalyzer` driven by MediaPipe SelfieSegmenter, `HandTrackingAnalyzer` driven by MediaPipe HandLandmarker). A third opt-in pair (`VoiceActivityAnalyzer` + `AudioMouthSyncAnalyzer`) consumes microphone capture for anti-replay defence. The bundle also runs the three Aysenur-authored gates (face-usability, illumination, critical-region-visibility), `MultiClassFuser` + `HybridFusionEvaluator`, `AntispoofPipelineAssembler`, `SessionEngine`, and a substantially extended `LivenessProver` whose passive proof score now spans **fifteen evidence axes** with a 185-point ceiling. Per-frame budget on a Pixel-class Android device (Brave / Chrome Mobile) sustains 6.7–9.5 fps end-to-end with all default analyzers enabled; on desktop Chrome with the WebGPU execution provider the same pipeline runs at 25–30 fps. All inference runs entirely client-side via `onnxruntime-web` and `@mediapipe/tasks-vision`. Hand-rolled equivalents replace cv2 ops (Sobel, Laplacian, Gabor, CLAHE → global histeq, RGB→HSV/YCrCb/Lab) so the bundle ships dependency-free of OpenCV.js.

A continuous-proctoring property emerges from the rolling-window design of the fifteen proof axes: thirteen of them decay to zero naturally if their underlying signal goes stale, which catches the *live-then-static* attack class — a user who shows their real face for the first minute and then swaps to a printed photo — without requiring an active challenge. Two port-correctness details surfaced during the browser implementation are worth noting because they apply to any future re-implementation: MediaPipe's facial transformation matrix is delivered in column-major order (OpenGL convention; reading it as row-major silently always yields tz = 0 because index 11 falls in the SE(3) bottom row); and the `eye_motion_points` / `mouth_motion_points` axes must be sourced from face-relative blendshape stddevs rather than 2D landmark variance, otherwise head rotation inflates per-region landmark motion purely because of image-plane projection.

## 10.2 The Python reference implementation

The Python implementation in `src/` is the **starting point for rapid development**, not the production target. It was written first because calibration sweeps, dataset adapters, and the ISO/IEC 30107-3 benchmark harness all run faster outside the browser, and because the original FIVUCSAS server-side liveness pipeline (where this work was incubated) was a Python service. We kept it because it remains the right tool for two distinct jobs: (a) running the formal cross-dataset evaluation in §6–§8 (a single `tests/benchmark/run.py` invocation over 5,119 samples), and (b) functioning as the calibration source-of-truth for the browser bundle so any change to the fusion weights or peak-sensitive aggregator can be re-validated end-to-end against the published headline numbers before it ships to clients.

The browser bundle is intentionally **calibration-equivalent** to the Python reference for the twelve analyzers present in both: the same paper-cited weights (texture and moire at 0.0 after the §5.3 anti-correlation finding; `device_boundary` and `micro_tremor` reduced to 0.5 after the §8.2 zero-shot LOO findings; `background_grid` held at 1.5 as the sole transferable positive contributor) ship in `DEFAULT_ANALYZER_WEIGHTS` on both sides, and the same session-verdict + peak-sensitive aggregation runs verbatim. The hand-rolled cv2 equivalents in the browser are documented inline in each ported file; algorithmic deviations (CLAHE → global histeq in three analyzers, reduced Gabor kernel in moire) are small enough to be absorbed by the analyzer-level normalisation but are not themselves the subject of a controlled accuracy delta study. The seven net-new analyzers the browser introduces (the blendshape and 3D-pose ones from §10.1) rely on data MediaPipe already produces but the Python source did not request; they extend the multi-signal evidence basis without modifying any existing weight or threshold. A `runCasiaFasdMicroBench` harness embedded in the browser tester exposes the per-device accuracy measurement as a one-click operation, so any operator can verify zero-shot AUC parity on their own device class without setting up a Python environment.

The Python desktop reference is described here as a deliberate engineering-process artefact rather than a paper-grade research contribution: the algorithmic claims of this paper are tied to the Python `src/` and the controlled-environment evaluation of §7–§8 because that is the test bench the cross-dataset numbers were produced on, while the seven browser-only multi-signal extensions (blendshape-derived analyzers, behavioural-pattern temporal-distribution axis, audio-mouth correlation, hand-presence anomaly) are documented here as future-work directions that the production browser deployment surfaces for empirical study but does not itself benchmark.

## Reproducibility

All numerical results in §7 and §8 are reproducible from public code at <https://github.com/Rollingcat-Software/spoof-detector> (MIT license, pinned to the release commit reported on the README badge). The Python reference implementation lives in `src/` (138 unit tests, CI-gated). The TypeScript browser bundle lives in `web/` (217 vitest tests, published as the npm package `@rollingcat/spoof-detector`). Dataset adapters for the public benchmarks are in `tests/benchmark/datasets/*.py` and currently cover CASIA-FASD, CelebA-Spoof, OULU-NPU, SiW and CASIA-SURF. Single-command reproduction: `python -m tests.benchmark.run --dataset <name>`. The calibration-sweep harness referenced in §5.4 is `tests/benchmark/calibration_sweep.py` (note: the shipped fuser weights are heuristic, not the output of a committed reproducible sweep — see §5.4); the leave-one-out ablation used in §8.2 is `tests/benchmark/ablation_leave_one_out.py`; the α-sweep used in §4.4.3 is `tests/benchmark/alpha_sweep.py`. All published CIs use `seed=42` and a stratified bootstrap at the two-tier resample convention of §11 (n_resamples=1500 for N ≤ 100 tiers, n_resamples=100 for the full large public sets). §11 maps every results table to its backing JSON; the §8.3 weight-configuration rows are reproduced by `python -m tests.benchmark.ablation_leave_one_out --mode weights`, which the harness now exposes.
