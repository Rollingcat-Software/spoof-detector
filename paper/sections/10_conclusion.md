# 10. Conclusion

We have presented `spoof-detector` v0.2.1, a session-based hybrid presentation-attack detection engine for the deployment contexts — proctoring, hosted KYC, regulated remote-onboarding — that the per-frame FAS literature does not directly serve. We have shown that the four contributions from §1 hold under controlled evaluation. We have presented a hybrid architecture (§4) that combines one strong per-frame discriminator (MiniFASNet ONNX, fuser weight 5.0) with twelve auxiliary analyzers across image and temporal modalities, fused under a calibrated multi-class voter and aggregated by a peak-sensitive 50/50 mean-plus-worst-decile session verdict (§4.4) designed to resist spoof-burst dilution. We have measured the principal empirical finding — that Laplacian-texture and Gabor-moire are anti-correlated on modern high-resolution capture (§5.3) — and shown via §8.3 that re-weighting those two analyzers from 1.0 to 0.1 alone recovers 0.017 of the 0.019 AUC gap between uniform and calibrated fusers, i.e. 89% of the calibration benefit captured by a single two-coefficient adjustment.

We have presented bootstrap-CI ISO/IEC 30107-3 evaluation on two zero-shot public benchmarks: AUC 0.9452 [0.9366, 0.9560] on CASIA-FASD (N=2,408) and AUC 0.7818 [0.7663, 0.7993] on CelebA-Spoof (N=2,611). The two confidence intervals are strictly separated by more than 4× their width, establishing the cross-dataset taxonomy effect at 95% confidence. We have additionally measured (§8.2) that two auxiliary analyzers calibrated on the in-house Marmara set actively harm zero-shot AUC on CASIA-FASD — `device_boundary` by 0.027 and `micro_tremor` by 0.021 — while `background_grid` is the lone in-house-calibrated auxiliary that transfers as a marginal positive contributor (AUC drops 0.014 when removed); together these motivate per-operator recalibration as a structural requirement of any cross-domain deployment.

We have released the engine, the calibration sweep harness, the leave-one-out ablation harness, the four-dataset adapters, and the ISO/IEC 30107-3 metrics implementation under MIT, alongside an iBeta PAD-Level-1 submission package pinned to the v0.2.1 release commit. The actionable artefact for the FAS community is the open benchmark harness itself: per-operator recalibration, leave-one-out diagnosis of harm-on-transfer, and bootstrap-CI ISO-compliant reporting are now one-command operations on any labelled FAS corpus. We expect the next round of public FAS benchmarks — and the next generation of cross-dataset robustness studies — to take advantage of that infrastructure.

## 10.1 Browser reference implementation

A second open-source deliverable accompanies the Python engine: a TypeScript port of the full pipeline published as `@rollingcat/spoof-detector` (npm) with a live, webcam-driven reference implementation at `https://fivucsas.com/amispoof/`. The browser bundle runs **ten** of the analyzers from §4.1 (MiniFASNet, screen-flicker, micro-tremor, blink/EAR, rPPG, device-boundary, landmark-variance, moire, texture, screen-replay), the three Aysenur-authored gates (face-usability, illumination, critical-region-visibility), `MultiClassFuser` + `HybridFusionEvaluator`, `AntispoofPipelineAssembler`, `SessionEngine`, and `LivenessProver` entirely client-side via `onnxruntime-web` (WASM EP, optional WebGPU EP with feature-detected fallback) and `@mediapipe/tasks-vision`. No frames are uploaded; no GPU server is required. Hand-rolled equivalents replace cv2 ops (Sobel, Laplacian, Gabor, CLAHE → global histeq, RGB→HSV/YCrCb/Lab) so the bundle ships dependency-free of OpenCV.js (~125 kB ESM, 33 kB gzip).

The browser port is intentionally calibration-equivalent to the Python `src/`: the same paper-cited weights (texture and moire at 0.0 after the §5.3 anti-correlation finding; `device_boundary` and `micro_tremor` reduced to 0.5 after the §8.2 zero-shot LOO findings; `background_grid` held at 1.5 as the sole transferable positive contributor) ship in `DEFAULT_ANALYZER_WEIGHTS`, and the same session-verdict + peak-sensitive aggregation runs verbatim. The hand-rolled cv2 equivalents are documented inline in each ported file; algorithmic deviations (CLAHE → global histeq in three analyzers, reduced Gabor kernel in moire) are small enough to be absorbed by the analyzer-level normalisation but are not yet themselves the subject of a controlled accuracy delta study. A `runCasiaFasdMicroBench` harness embedded in the browser tester exposes the per-device accuracy measurement as a one-click operation, so any operator can verify zero-shot AUC parity on their own device class without setting up a Python environment. We treat the browser port as a deployment artefact rather than a paper-grade research contribution; the algorithmic claims of this paper are tied to the Python `src/` and the controlled-environment evaluation of §7–§8.
